# 20: The forgotten harbor and the ending

**What to build:** The forgotten harbor beyond the Guardian, with the old vessel. Interacting with the vessel prepares it, and the hero sails home. Departing is the game's only point of no return, so it asks for confirmation. A brief ending and a credits screen follow, then the title screen. There is no postgame. Art stays placeholder until the art tickets.

**Blocked by:** 19: The Guardian

**Status:** resolved

**Spec stories:** 15, 17-19, 27

- [x] The harbor section of the castle area (completing ticket 19's stub) contains the old vessel. Interacting with it prepares it (default: one interaction; extra fetch items are optional), and then the hero can depart.
- [x] Departing asks for confirmation that the adventure ends. Until the player confirms, the hero can backtrack anywhere in the island; after confirming, nothing can be revisited.
- [x] A brief ending text (earnest but light) and a credits screen follow. The credits list the game's credits and every attribution required by the licences of the assets used, taken from the asset licence record, and the screen then returns to the title.
- [x] After the ending the session is at the title screen with no postgame. The last autosave is left untouched.
- [x] Content validation requires exactly one vessel, and that the harbor is reachable only through the defeated Guardian.
- [x] Tests at the Game core's interface cover that the ending is reachable only after the Guardian is defeated, that departure needs confirmation, that departure ends the session and returns to the title state, and that the save is left untouched.
- [x] Reviewer play check (manual, in a browser): defeat the Guardian, prepare the vessel, decline the departure and backtrack, then depart, read the ending and credits, and land on the title screen.

### What was built

- **The vessel** (`vessel-content.ts`, `departure.ts`, `definitions.ts`, `content.ts`, `interactables.ts`, `obstacles.ts`, `consumable-rollback.ts`, `state.ts`, `view.ts`). A new kind of interactable, `vessel` (`id` and `tile`), solid like a chest. Interact on one that is not prepared prepares it and tells `vessel-prepared`, once. Interact on one that is prepared opens the question whether to sail home: `PlayState.departure` holds the vessel's id, and `GameView.departure` is `{ vesselId }` or `null`. That the vessel is prepared is kept like where a lever stands, so a defeat does not undo it; `VesselView.prepared` is for the adapter.
- **The question and its two answers** (`departure.ts`, `game.ts`, `pause.ts`, `interface.ts`). While the question is open the game is paused, as it is for an open page, and the question is part of the snapshot. `cancel-departure` closes it and leaves everything as it was, the vessel still prepared. `confirm-departure` ends the game: its one event is `ending-reached`, carrying the lines of the ending and of the credits, and the core is at the title afterwards, as before the first New Game. Nothing can be revisited and there is no postgame. The core writes nothing, so the last autosave is as it was. Both commands are ignored when no question is open and at the title.
- **The ending and the credits are content** (`ContentDefinition.ending`, 1 to 4 lines, and `credits`, 1 or more, both or neither, none blank; `src/content/ending.ts`, passed on in `src/content/index.ts`). The ending is four short lines: the storm gone and the sea calm, the old vessel, home with sword and armor, a story to tell. The credits are the title, the tools (Phaser, TypeScript and Vite), the credits text of the one asset that `docs/assets/licences.md` marks as in use (the Ninja Adventure pack by Pixel-Boy and AAA, CC0, with its link) and thanks.
- **The harbor** (`castle.ts`). The quay that ticket 19 left (sand in columns 85 to 88 and rows 10 to 12, water in 89 and 90, behind `harbor-gate` at (84, 11)) now holds `old-vessel` at (88, 11), at the water's edge in the middle row. The hero uses it from (87, 11), or from any tile that touches it.
- **What loading refuses** (`vessel-content.ts`, `guardian-content.ts`, `device-routes.ts`). Always: a vessel in content with no ending and credits; a second vessel (the message names both); a vessel that the hero's way needs a key, a lever or a door for. With `requireGuardian`, which the game's content sets: no vessel at all; a vessel in another Adventure Area than the Guardian's; a vessel that the hero can walk up to while the Guardian's gates are shut (every other door and gate open); a vessel that it cannot walk up to even with everything open. To make that checkable the route check lets a Guardian's gate pass in its shut case: the Guardian's defeat is the way, and no key, lever or door, so a Checkpoint or a vessel in the harbor is on the route. A `Place` has `tiles` now, since the hero uses a vessel from any of the four tiles that touch it.

Card drawing and layout are shared with the opening (`opening-card.ts`, refactored). The first Interact gives the notice THE VESSEL IS READY / PRESS E AGAIN TO SAIL, for three seconds. The vessel is a flat shape, with a lighter sail once prepared (`art.ts`). The game refuses to start with an ending or credits line that the pixel font cannot draw (`undrawableIn`, `requireDrawableEnding`).

### Decisions and defaults

- **Flagged for the user (a default, not in the spec): preparing is one Interact, and the next Interact asks.** The ticket's default is one interaction and no fetch items, and the spec leaves open what preparing involves. The first Interact on the vessel tells the player that it is ready and which key sails it; the second opens the question. So the departure takes two deliberate presses and then an answer, and a hero who walks away and comes back finds the vessel still prepared.
- **Declining changes nothing.** The question closes, the vessel stays prepared, the hero is where it was and may walk the whole island; the next Interact asks again. Esc is "stay", Enter is "sail", and nothing else answers.
- **No autosave is added for the vessel.** Preparing it, the question and sailing save nothing (criterion 4: the last autosave stands). The consequence is that a tab closed after preparing the vessel resumes from the autosave of the Guardian's victory, with the vessel not yet prepared: one more Interact.
- **Old saves become incompatible** (version 3 to 4), as at tickets 13 and 19: a player with a slot from before this ticket starts a New Game.
- **What a save is checked for** (`saved-game.ts`): `state.departure` is `null` or the id of a vessel of this game, in the area the hero is in, that is prepared; and a vessel that is prepared needs every Guardian whose gate lies in the vessel's area to have fallen. That last rule is an approximation: content that is not asked to have a Guardian, with a Guardian's gate in the vessel's area that shuts nothing off, would get a vessel that was legitimately prepared named damaged. The game's content, and any content that passes `requireGuardian`, are not affected: there the vessel is out of reach until the Guardian has fallen, so the rule is true. The wording of these messages, and of the loader's, is mine.
- **`requireGuardian` keeps its name** though it asks for the vessel now too: the flag is ticket 19's, and the vessel's rules need the Guardian's gate to mean anything.
- **The ending and the credits are content, carried by the event**, so that the adapter holds no text and the core knows no screen. The ending is 1 to 4 lines because a card is a screen; the credits are as long as they need to be and are cut into as many cards.
- **The credits name the title, the tools, the pack and give thanks; no person is named.** A comment in `ending.ts` says where the author's line goes, for the user to write. The pack's line is the "Credits text" of its entry in the record, which calls it a courtesy and asks for the link. `tests/the-credits.test.ts` reads `docs/assets/licences.md` from disk and asks for the credits text of every entry that is in use: a test that reads a document, as `final-art.test.ts` reads the pictures, which is a seam drift for the user's veto.
- **The font draws capitals** (lowercase comes out as capitals) and no `;`, `&`, quotes or accents, so the credits read in capitals. The game refuses to start with a line that cannot be drawn, and a test names the character and the line.
- **Enter turns the cards one at a time**: the ending, then the credits, then the title. No other key does anything on them and nothing skips.
- **The vessel is solid**, so `tests/routes.ts` knows it as it knows a chest, and the hero uses it from the ground beside it.
- **A held Enter was taken for a new press by the next scene** (found in the smoke). Phaser makes new Key objects for each scene, and a new Key counts the first auto-repeat keydown as a press, so a held Enter went from the answer of the question through the ending page to the credits, and from the credits to Continue. `pressedAgain` in `player-input.ts`, which every menu key and stat key goes through, now ignores a keydown that is an auto-repeat (red first: 7 tests, then the fix). It also fixes what was already so from the title to the card of the opening, where a held Enter on New Game took the card away at once (seen in the smoke before and after). One edge: a real press and its auto-repeat inside one frame of half a second or more would lose the press; a frame is about 16 ms.
- **Adapter tests.** As the README says of `fall`, `camera`, `opening-card` and the other pure pieces of the adapter, the new pure pieces are tested directly with worked-out numbers: `ending-pages`, `departure-menu`, `waiting`, `feedback`, `notice`, `pixel-font`, `play-start`, the overlay's panel and `pressedAgain`. Three of the files (`departure-panel`, `notice-lines`, `player-input`) have to mock Phaser, which the modules import at runtime and the node environment cannot load: a stub with an empty default, and in `player-input.test.ts` fake keys with a copy of Phaser's `JustDown`. These are the first tests of the suite that mock Phaser. ADR 0002 names three exceptions only, and the suite had already gone past them; the user may want to say whether this is wanted.
- **Not done, on purpose**: no sound, no art (the vessel and the cards are flat), no skipping, no name in the credits, no extra fetch items.
- **The Guardian's numbers and everything else that ticket 19 set are untouched.** The spec and the ticket do not disagree on anything this ticket touches.
