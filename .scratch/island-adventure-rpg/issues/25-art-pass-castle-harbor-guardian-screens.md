### What was built

- **The castle's ground** (`terrain-plan.ts`, still pure). As in 24, a `TerrainLook` names sheets laid out like the standard ones, so a look changes the picture of a frame and never which frame goes where or what is solid; `lookOf('castle')` gives the castle seven sheets of its own: `castle-floor` (dark flagstones with chips and cracks, and moss where they meet the grass), `castle-grass` (moss on stone), `castle-wall` (masonry: lip, face, foot), `castle-pillars`, `castle-water` (grey-green, with worn stone for a shore), `castle-dock` and `castle-fountain`. Three planner rules are new and are the castle's alone (the other looks have no slot for them): a rock with no rock above it and none below it is a **pillar** (four variants chosen by position: whole, mossy, broken, cracked; 18 on the shipped map, the arena's four among them; still solid, as authored); a block of rock three tiles by three with no rock in the ring round it is the **fountain**, found by its shape as `findTreeBlocks` and `findWrecks` find theirs (the one in the garden, columns 34 to 36, rows 11 to 13); and sand within four tiles of the castle's water, in any direction, is the **quay** (`dockFrame`: planks in six frames, plain twice, mossy twice, weeds and a split board, mixed by position; columns 85 to 88, rows 10 to 12). The plan of each of the four earlier areas is identical to the tip's (below).
- **The four undead** (`monster-castle-skeleton`, `-ghoul`, `-wraith`, `-knight`, 16 px cells): sheets of the same make as 23's and 24's (four facings by four frames of a walk, a row of a white hit shape, a row of a red wind-up), so `monster-sprites.ts` and its table (walk, idle, shake, flash, dash ghosts, puff) work on them as they are. Ids, numbers and hit boxes are the content's.
- **The Guardian** (`monster-castle-guardian`: cells of 96 by 96, four facings of six walk frames, a white row and a red row): the pack's Giant Blue Samurai (a figure 70 pixels wide) made an empty suit of armour in pale teal and ivory with lamp-cyan knots. It is the one big pale thing in the game, against warm grey stone and 16 px dark, green or violet undead. Its body (hit box) is the content's 1.6 tiles; its picture is bigger, as the brief says, on the same centre.
- **The harbor and the old vessel.** The quay is the planks of `castle-dock` (weathered brown, moss in the seams, weeds, a split board) beside the grey-green water. The old vessel (`prop-vessel`, two frames of 48 by 112) lies in the water at the quay's end with its bow to the north and its side to the planks: the pack's boat (a tile shorter, in old wood, a mended stern, moss on the gunwale), a mast, and the pack's sail, furled on the mast when the vessel is found and hoisted once it is prepared. `prop-sprites.ts` draws it (a `vessel` case in `show()`, and `vesselFoot`); the flat `drawVessel` of `art.ts` is gone, the `vessel` case of `drawInteractable` is a bare return like the gate's and the lever's, and the switch still ends in `satisfies never`.
- **The title.** `backdrop-title` (320 by 192, the size of the canvas, so nothing is scaled at run time): sky, sea, the shore, the wreck, the hero seen from behind, a crab and the old castle low on the horizon. Over it the name of the game in big letters (`title-layout.ts`, pure: the project's own pixel font at the biggest whole scale of 4, 3 and 2 that fits with 12 px at each side, which is 3; the title colour with a brown extrusion and a dark outline, drawn by `scaledText` of `ui-layer.ts`) and the menu window under it.
- **The ending and the credits.** `backdrop-ending` (320 by 192): an evening, the sun half set, a calm sea with the sun's path, the island with its castle on the horizon, the old vessel far off under sail with its wake, and the planks of the quay along the foot. The horizon is high so that the cards cover the middle; `EndingScene` puts the picture at `DEPTH.backdrop` (290) behind them. `layoutEnding`, `drawCard` and the cards, and so the credits' text and what is credited, are untouched. The opening card stays on black: it fades into the game.
- **The Automap's maps** (`automap-palette.ts`, used by `automap-page.ts`): each colour is one of the pack's 53 palette colours, chosen per area for water, rock, sand, grass, wreckage and tree (the coast and the woods alike, the mountain with a slate rock, the cave dim, the castle in stone and sand), and the exits and the unexplored ground have colours of their own. `automap-layout.ts` and what the page shows are untouched; `terrainColour` stays as the flat-art fallback.
- **Records and the switch.** 15 pictures are new in `public\art\` (seven of the castle's ground, five of undead and Guardian, the vessel and the two backdrops), each in `docs\assets\licences.md` ("Last updated", the A1 row, one entry each under "Used so far (ticket 25, composed pictures)", and the Automap's colours), and `docs\assets\compose-art.py` (+750 lines) makes them again: a full run re-made all 51 composed pictures of 23, 24 and 25 with no difference from `public\art`. `FINAL_ART_AREAS` is now `shipwreck-coast`, `woods`, `mountain`, `cave`, `castle`, last, when every picture existed; `tests\final-art.test.ts` took the new area by itself. ### Decisions and defaults

- **Stand-ins (named, as the brief asks).** The skeleton is the pack's own, kept as it is. The ghoul is the pack's beast made a sickly pale green (pale so that it shows on the dark flagstones); the wraith is the pack's spirit made violet; the knight, the tougher variant, is the pack's gold knight tarnished (neither the pack's gold nor the navy of 24's veteran). The Guardian is the pack's Giant Blue Samurai (G3, which the user approved). The vessel is the pack's boat and sail. Nothing was downloaded, and the 0x72 tileset (S4) was not needed: the vessel and the wreck (seen on the title) read well enough as they are. The one place where a ship seen from the side would help is the ending's (see Left for manual review).
- **Composed by recipe, not from the pack** (this project's own work in the pack's colours; the licence record says so for each): the pillars and the fountain (every pixel; the pack's `FX\Elemental\WaterPillar` was tried for the fountain's jet and read as a jar), the courses of blocks laid over the pack's masonry face, the chips, cracks and weeds of the floor, the moss, weeds and split board of the quay, the vessel's mast, furled sail, post with its line, mended stern and moss, the white and red rows of the monster sheets (as 23's recipe makes them), and, in the two backdrops, the skies, clouds, gulls, the sun, the sea and its ripples, the islands with their castles, the small ship of the ending and the posts of the quay. The big letters of the title are the project's own pixel font, scaled: the pack's non-commercial font and bitmap fonts are not used.
- **The Guardian's poses.** A boss that never turns its back: the pack draws it from the front only, so up is the mirror of down; left and right are the walk frames leaning three pixels each way; its idle is the slow sway of 23's table. The brief suggested the white row from the pack's `Hit` and the red row from the first frames of its attacks; I made the white row as for every monster (a white shape of the walk frames) and the red row from the second frame of the pack's two charges (the lunge, a blade raised high) in red. The pack's idle, hit and sweep frames, and the first and the last of the charges, are unused: the view has no pose for a strike, so nowhere to show them. The brief's luminance gradient map with a 1 px glow, and its spectral touches (`FX\Magic\Spirit`, `FX\Smoke`), are not done: the colour swap (the pack's blue, grey-green and dark made pale teals, its gold ivory, its orange and red lamp-cyan) already sets the Guardian apart from the stone and from every undead on the screenshots, and anything added to a 96 px picture is one more thing for a telegraph to compete with. Adding them would be a change in `compose-art.py` alone.
- **Telegraphs: nothing was changed, and the Guardian's stay under its picture** (`DEPTH.telegraph`, under the monsters, as for all the others). The brief feared that a big picture would hide a zone, so I looked at each pattern at the real size with the Guardian where it winds up from (the middle of the arena, against each of the four walls, in the gateway's mouth), at the start, the middle and the end of the wind-up, and with the hero inside and outside the zone (`t25-g-*.png`, `t25-gw-*.png`): the sweep's square (1.4 tiles out on every side) shows all round the figure, which is not a square; the charge's lane runs from the Guardian to the hero; the smite's square on the hero's spot shows beside the figure at each distance I tried; and the red lunge pose says "now" over the zone. A prototype that drew the zones above the picture turned the pale armour into a red blob and hid the red pose, so I dropped it. The Guardian has no projectile or other effect: its three zones are all it shows. Its hit flash, its defeat and the gate opening in the same step were looked at too (`04-arena-hit`, `05-arena-defeat` and `06-gate-open` of the real-key route).
- **The health bar** goes above the picture, as the brief says: above the 96 px cell, about two tiles over the Guardian's head. At the arena's north wall with the hero in melee (1.5 tiles away) it stays on the screen, over the masonry, and reads. ### How to run it

- `npm.cmd run dev` and open the printed URL (add `?debug` for `window.gameCore`). Press Enter on the title and on the opening card and play through the coast, the woods, the mountain and the cave to the castle; the Guardian waits in the arena east of the gateway (row 11), and the gate to the harbor opens when it falls. At the quay press E on the vessel (the notice THE VESSEL IS READY), E again for the question and Enter to sail; the ending and the credits follow, and Enter on the last page returns to the title. With `?debug` the hero can be placed by patching the snapshot, as the scratch scripts do.
- The production output: `npm.cmd run build`, then `npx.cmd vite preview --port <port> --strictPort` (the pictures of `public\art` reach `dist`).
- To make the pictures again: `python docs\assets\compose-art.py --pack "<the folder Ninja Adventure - Asset Pack>"` (Python 3 with Pillow; I ran Python 3.14 with Pillow 12), or add names after it to make only those (for example `prop-vessel castle-dock`). To give another area its art, see the README's Art section.
- The scratch scripts and screenshots are outside the repository: `.worktrees\_notes\t25-work\` (`scenes25.mjs`, `run25.mjs`, `place.js`, `plan-seq25.mjs`, the comparison scripts, `mutations.md`, `screens-list.md`) and `.worktrees\_notes\t25-*.png`.
