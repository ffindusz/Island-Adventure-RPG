# 12: Charger and ranged enemies

**What to build:** Two more reusable enemy behaviors alongside the basic chaser: a charger that telegraphs and then dashes in a straight line, and a ranged attacker that telegraphs and then fires a projectile along a straight line. Both are avoidable by positioning alone. Behaviors are chosen and tuned through content data, so differently looking monsters with different stats reuse them across Adventure Areas, including tougher variants for the few demanding fights.

**Blocked by:** 06: The bow: Ammunition and quick-switching

**Status:** resolved

**Spec stories:** 42, 51, 52

- [x] Charger: a visible wind-up, then a straight-line dash that stops at solid terrain, then a short recovery during which it can be hit freely. Damage lands only if the hero is in the dash's path.
- [x] Ranged attacker: a visible wind-up, then a projectile fired in a straight line toward where the hero stood when it fired. The projectile damages the hero on contact and is stopped by solid terrain. Whether it keeps a preferred distance or stands still is a data setting.
- [x] Monster projectiles reuse the projectile mechanism from the bow.
- [x] No attack is unavoidable: tests show each can be avoided by moving during the wind-up.
- [x] Adding a monster that reuses a behavior needs only content data (for example speed, wind-up, range, damage, Health, Experience). A tougher variant is a data change.
- [x] The placeholder areas contain a monster of each behavior.
- [x] Tests at the Game core's interface cover timing, the dash path and terrain stop, projectile travel, hit and block, avoidance, and recovery.
- [x] Reviewer play check (manual, in a browser): fight a charger and a ranged attacker, avoiding both by moving.

### What was built

- Two behaviors in the registry beside `chaser`: `charger` (`src\core\charger.ts`, with `lanes.ts` for the lane and `sweep.ts` for moving a body until it meets a target) and `ranged` (`src\core\ranged.ts`). Each is a member of the `MonsterTypeDefinition` union with a `loadMonsterType` case that checks its numbers, so the compiler listed every place that needed one. Walking and aiming that the behaviors share moved into `monster-movement.ts`; the chaser uses them too and its tests did not change.
- A charger notices the hero, walks sideways until its lane (the strip in front of it, as wide as its body, cut short by solid terrain and chests) takes the hero in, winds up with the lane locked and shown, dashes down it, and recovers standing still. A ranged attacker notices the hero, walks to its preferred distance if its type has one, winds up with a flash on its own body, fires one projectile along its row or column toward where the hero is at that moment, and recovers.
- A monster's projectile is an arrow in `PlayState.arrows` with a `shotBy`: flown by the same `flyArrows`, stopped by the same things, bound to its area and cleared in the same places as the hero's. It hurts the hero through `hurtHero`, so Defense, `hero-hit` and defeat apply. The view's `ArrowView.shooter` (`'hero'` or `'monster'`) tells them apart. New event `monster-fired`; a dash launches with `monster-attacked` whose zone is the lane; the view's monster actions gained `dashing`.
- Content: `boar` (a charger) and `spitter` (a ranged attacker that keeps a distance) in `src\content\monsters.ts`, one of each in the coast and in the trail, away from the entrances and the start.
- Adapter (`art.ts`, `play-scene.ts`, small edits): the lane is the telegraph (`drawTelegraph`) and flashes with a trail while the dash runs; the shooter flashes on its body while it winds up; a monster's projectile is an orb with a trail, unlike the hero's arrows. The HUD is untouched. README: new section "Chargers and ranged attackers".
- 144 new tests in 9 new files (`charger`, `charger-lining-up`, `charger-avoidance`, `ranged-attacker`, `ranged-keeping-distance`, `projectile`, `ranged-avoidance`, `enemy-content`, `shipped-enemies`) on one arena in `tests\enemy-fixtures.ts`, all at the Game core's interface. ### Decisions and defaults

- How the charger is avoided: the lane is locked when the wind-up starts and is shown for all of it, so the hero avoids the charger by leaving the strip during the wind-up (500 ms for the test charger, 700 ms for the boar); the dash runs on whether or not the hero is still there and hurts only a hero in its way. Tests: out of the lane up and down, at the very last step of the wind-up, and even after the dash is launched while it is still far; too late is hurt; a hero right beside the lane is not. And the other half: a hero who stays takes the hand-worked damage after Defense, the last Health defeats and the fight starts over, and a hit on the charger does not interrupt it.
- How the ranged attacker is avoided: its projectile is aimed when it is fired and flies along the shooter's own row or column, slower than the hero's walk (3.75 against 5 tiles a second in the tests, 4 against 5 for the spitter). The hero avoids it by being off that row and column when the shot is fired, which it can do during the wind-up, or by stepping aside from the shot in the air until the last three steps before it arrives. Tests: leaving up or down costs nothing; two steps are not enough; sidestepping in the air; one step too late is hurt; the aim is taken at the shot and not at the start of the wind-up; a hero who walks into the line after the shot is hit; a hero who stays takes the damage.
- The aim is an axis, not an angle: from the shooter's centre toward the axis the hero is farther away on, a tie going horizontal, like the hero's bow. A hero off that row and column is missed. The ticket says the shot goes toward where the hero stood when it fired, and the spec asks that attacks be telegraphed and avoided by moving; both hold, but the line is not known until the shot, so the telegraph is a flash on the shooter's body (`winding-up` with its own body as the `zone`) that says a shot is coming, not a line that shows where. A ranged line-up like the charger's would make the line readable: a follow-up.
- Terrain and chests stop a dash and a projectile (a chest shortens the lane that is shown). A projectile flies through monsters and hurts none. A dash is blocked by another monster, which shields the hero behind it, and by the hero, whom it hurts once and stops against. Contact counts as a hit along the way of flight; edges that only meet sideways do not (`projectile.test.ts`: a hero whose top edge only meets the projectile's bottom edge is flown by).
- Recovery means the monster stands still and can be hit freely. A hit never interrupts a wind-up, a dash or a recovery (no flinch), so every timeline is the same whether or not the hero strikes.
- The charger starts its wind-up when the hero's body overlaps its lane by any amount and the hero is within its noticing distance. A stricter centred alignment is a follow-up. Its lane is cut by terrain and chests only. A charger whose speed is 0 stays idle until the hero steps into its lane.
- `keepsDistance: { preferredDistance, speedTilesPerSecond }` is optional on a ranged type: that is the data setting for "keeps a distance or stands still". The distance is the gap between the two bodies along the axis they are farther apart on; the keeper walks nearer when the gap is larger, backs away when it is smaller, in steps cut to what is left, and winds up once at the distance. A retreat that cannot move (a wall at its back) winds up where it stands; an approach that is blocked stays `chasing` and never fires. The shipped spitter keeps a distance; the stand-still variant is covered by tests only.
- A defeat by a projectile ends the step there: `flyArrows` clears the arrows in flight and a guard after it in `game.ts` ends the step, so the monsters, restarted by the defeat, act first in the next one. Ticket 09 changes where a defeat goes, not that it clears the arrows: keep that guard when merging. When the hero goes through an exit every monster goes idle (a wind-up or a dash in progress starts over when it comes back) and the arrows in flight are gone.
- A projectile keeps its shooter's monster type (`shotBy.monsterType`), so it flies on when its shooter is defeated. `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` stays 1: an arrow without `shotBy` is the hero's, and a dash and a wind-up restore with the lane that was locked (tested).
- Placeholder numbers (every one is for the tuning pass). Boar: Health 4, size 0.85, damage 6 (5 after the hero's Defense of 1), line-up speed 2 tiles a second, noticing 4, wind-up 700 ms, dash 12 tiles a second over 6 tiles, recovery 1000 ms, 8 Experience. Spitter: Health 2, size 0.6, noticing 5, wind-up 800 ms, recovery 1500 ms, projectile 4 damage (3 after Defense) at 4 tiles a second over 6 tiles, size 0.3, keeps 3 tiles at 2 tiles a second, 6 Experience. Placement: coast boar (15,10) and spitter (12,1), trail boar (10,15) and spitter (20,2), none near an entrance or the start. `shipped-enemies.test.ts` pins that no shipped monster notices a hero standing on an entrance or the start tile, that each area has one of each, that the spitter's projectile is slower than the hero's walk and that the boar's wind-up is long enough to leave its lane.

### Suggested follow-ups

- A ranged line-up like the charger's: the shooter walks to the hero's row or column before it winds up, so that its line is readable and it is not mostly a miss from a distance.
- A stricter charger alignment (the hero's centre in the lane, not any overlap).
- `continue` does no validation, so a snapshot holding a projectile whose monster type has since left the content would throw in `projectileOf` (`src\core\arrows.ts`). The same holds for a saved monster of a removed type, as before.
- GLOSSARY.md lacks the terms telegraph, wind-up, recovery (in the monsters' sense), noticing distance, dash, lane, projectile, behavior, charger and ranged attacker. The code, the tests and the README use them as ticket 03 did.
